# Roadmap

Settled 2026-10-24 in a design grilling session; on 2026-10-30 trending moved ahead of live and sports, and its details were settled; on 2026-11-01 live and sports' details were settled; on 2026-11-03 polish's details were settled; on 2026-11-06 paying was settled, and leaving TMDB was split out as its own milestone; on 2026-11-07 for everyone's details were settled, and leaving TMDB was settled. Terms are defined in [CONTEXT.md](../CONTEXT.md); hard-to-reverse choices are in [docs/adr](adr/). Big decisions are made now for the whole roadmap; feature details are settled just before each milestone.

Priorities, in order: a fast catalog with the best Version in the Viewer's language, subtitles and smooth playback of every format on a nice interface; then trending, so Home is worth opening every day; then live sports; then visual polish.

## Surveyed Provider (2026-10-24)

Re-run with `python3 -I scripts/survey.py` (reads `.env`, writes to the git-ignored `.survey/`).

- Connection limit: 1. On 2026-10-29 it wasn't enforced within one home network: a second stream from a Mac on the stick's network was served while the stick played, so the refusal code is still unknown ([#82](https://github.com/victortrinh/better-iptv/issues/82)). `ProviderLoadErrorPolicy` keeps retrying 403 and 429 in case it is one of those. A 407, which the Provider sent when a Channel's TS request was refused, reaches the player as a `ProtocolException` and is final, so the HLS Backup starts at once ([#108](https://github.com/victortrinh/better-iptv/issues/108)).
- 144,031 movie Listings (54,658 Titles), 36,252 show Listings (14,818 Titles), 45,710 live Listings; 22 MB compressed.
- TMDB ID on 94% of movie and show Listings.
- Names follow `PREFIX - Title (Year)` with 105 distinct Prefixes; Category names add hints ("MULTI-SUBS", "Dolby Vision", "DOLBY AUDIO", "VOSTFR").
- Movie details rarely list Tracks; Episode details list resolution and the first audio Track's language.
- 6,725 Event channels by a broad count; on the survey day 4,550 of them were dated and live or upcoming, 965 marked finished and 6,424 empty slots. The TV guide covers 15% of Channels (6,689 Listings, 3,498 guide ids, generic titles such as "Live: NHL Hockey") and no Event channel. 919 Channels have 3 or 7 days of catch-up. Merging Channel variants (quality words and Tier tags stripped) collapses 12,913 Listings into 4,086 Channels; 5,053 Sports replays, almost all `SOC - Team at Team dd.mm.yyyy`.

## M0: Foundations

- One repo: app, cloud, scripts.
- Automatic APK builds; install on the stick over Wi-Fi with adb.
- Sync: stream the catalog into the device database.
- Prefix table for the surveyed Provider, covering all ~25 languages.
- Tests run against a snapshot of the real catalog.
- Measure Sync and Header read times on the stick itself.

## M5: For everyone

Settled 2026-11-07 in a grilling session against the code, the cloud (ADRs 0001–0013), the 2026-10-24 survey and the paying grilling of 2026-11-06, which settled the parts Paying depends on (kept here as they were). The goal: any household, on any Provider, with its own history, on every TV in the house. The spec is [#425](https://github.com/victortrinh/better-iptv/issues/425). Hard-to-reverse choices are in [ADR 0014](adr/0014-provider-table-learned-from-header-reads.md) and [ADR 0015](adr/0015-accounts-by-magic-link-on-the-device-token.md); the terms are in CONTEXT.md under Provider catalog and Household.

- **Any Provider: the Prefix table splits in two.** The **Base table** ships with the app and holds the conventions most Xtream panels share: the plain language codes (`EN`, `FR`, `DE`, `QC`…), the `4K-xx` and `xx-4K` combos, the Source codes (`NF`, `D+`, `AMZ`, `A+`, `MAX`…), the Category hints, the Clutter patterns, the Tier tags, the quality words and the genre words. The **Provider table** is one Provider's own, served by the cloud under the Provider's host hash (the hash devices already send with Header reads and unknown Prefixes). It has two kinds of line. *Hand lines* live in the repo, one file per Provider hash under `cloud/provider-tables/`, in the Base table's format, loaded into D1 on deploy: the surveyed Provider's file is today's table minus what moved to Base, so its 13 typos (`CD`, `EQC`, `IIR`, `R`, `AL`, `K-FR`, `4L-PL`, `4M-AMZ`, `S0C`, `INKD`, `BI`, `N`, `AC`) and its own codes (`IN-xx`, `SO-IN`, `AF-EN`, `EN-TOP`, `DE-JO`, `TOP` as Blu-ray, `SC`/`AS`/`AF`) regress nothing for the household. *Learned lines* are what Header reads on that Provider taught (below). Precedence: a hand line over a learned line over Base. A Provider line for a key Base has overrides it; an empty rule (`A+ |`) switches a Base meaning off; `[event time zone]` replaces Base's; every other section adds. No delete syntax.
- **The device fetches the Provider table at the start of every Sync** (`POST /v1/provider-table` with the hash), keeps it in the catalog database, and the Sync describes Listings with Base plus Provider table, so a table change takes effect at the next Sync (the 6-hour stale check), where Clutter, Sports replays and the Matching language are fixed; Version badges follow live, since attributes are computed on read. No table yet, or the cloud out of reach: Base alone, and the last table kept stays in use.
- **Learning.** A file-read report (`POST /v1/file-reads`, 50 Listings a batch, from Header reads and Playback alike) gains the Listing's Prefix and the Category hints that matched (`"prefix": "SC", "hints": ["MULTI-SUBS"]`): a code and a hint word, never a Title name, so ADR 0001's line holds. The cloud tallies per Provider and Prefix and learns an attribute once about 10 reads agree at 90% or more (all of them when the Prefix has fewer than 10 Listings, which the unknown-Prefix report counts), thresholds tuned in the cloud. *Language* is learned only when 90% of reads carry a single audio language and it is the same; when most reads carry two or more audio languages the Prefix learns **multi-audio**, which the device treats as Untagged (Original audio Likely), since an `NF` file held 7 audio Tracks and would otherwise learn English. *Quality* learns from the picture height (4K at or above 2160 lines, HD at or above 720), *HDR* and *Dolby audio* from what the file states. Sources, Clutter, Tier tags, subtitles-culture meanings (`SC`), the Event time zone and country aliases can't be learned: Base's defaults stand, with hand lines. A learned attribute shows as Likely, the outlined badge; there is no third certainty level. Learned lines are recomputed on each report: one that drops under the bar is withdrawn and flagged. Reads that disagree (`SC` 55% Swedish, 45% Danish) learn nothing and the weekly unknown-Prefixes issue lists the split for a hand line; a learned line that contradicts Base applies (it was measured) and the issue lists the contradiction. Every device reports its reads, Unlocked devices (M7) also receive others'; the learned table itself is free.
- **A new Provider.** Day one: Base alone, unknown Prefixes reported, their Versions "Language not named". The details page's reads and Background reading put Versions with unknown Prefixes first, then the Original-audio order; the pace stays the Viewer's setting. Week two: the Prefixes the Provider's Viewers opened are learned. The weekly issue gains a section per Provider seen, and a Sync also reports *candidate Tier tags*: a word before the colon whose removal makes a live Listing's name identical to another Listing's (`VIP: TNT SPORT 1 HD` and `GOLD: TNT SPORT 1 HD`), with counts, for the curator to accept; never applied by itself, since a wrong Tier tag merges two Channels.
- **Accounts.** An Account is an email address with a magic link, no password, and no Google or Apple sign-in. The TV's Settings row "Sign in" shows a QR and a 6-character Sign-in code (digits and capitals without 0/O/1/I, 10 minutes, 5 tries); the phone opens the page the Worker serves on the app's own domain, types the code and an email, and taps the magic link it receives (15 minutes, once); the TV, polling every 2 s while the screen shows, is signed in within seconds. An unknown email creates the Account. The device token stays the credential, bound to the Account ([ADR 0015](adr/0015-accounts-by-magic-link-on-the-device-token.md)): every endpoint keeps working, Settings' device list is the Account's devices (model, "Fire TV Stick 4K Max", and sign-in date), Sign out on any of them clears the link and the device goes anonymous at its next cloud contact, and M7's 3-device limit is a count. The page carries a one-paragraph privacy note (kept: the email, history, the device list; never the Provider login) and "Delete my account" behind a magic link, which deletes everything and sends every device back to anonymous. Email goes out through a transactional sender (Resend's free tier, 3,000 a month) from the domain; the cloud keeps the address and a hash of the magic token. Codes and registrations are rate-limited per IP by a Cloudflare rule. **No Trakt**: its terms couldn't be read, secondary sources say commercial apps need its approval and some endpoints need the user's Trakt VIP, M2 rejected its lists, and the Account already syncs history.
- **What the Account owns, what the device keeps.** Account, per profile: where the Viewer stopped and what they Watched, Favourites, Pinned teams, Leagues, "Not this game" marks, Content languages, Prefer original audio, App language, Play next automatically, the in-player choices per Title. Account, for the household: Region (it feeds the Charts and the League defaults, which are about where the TV is) and, in M7, the Purchase. Device: the Provider login (ADR 0001: each TV types it; no hand-off through the Account), the catalog, Quality cap, Text size, subtitle style, Background reading pace. History keyed by TMDB ID follows the person across Providers; Favourites, "Not this game" and the progress of what has no TMDB ID (Unmatched Titles, Sports replays, keyed by Listing) are kept per Provider hash. Signing in merges the device's local data into the Account (per Title, the latest change wins); signing out takes the Account's data off the device, which goes back to a blank anonymous state with its catalog and Provider login. A device that never signs in works as today, forever: sign-in buys sync and, in M7, the Purchase.
- **History sync.** Per Movie or Episode the change with the latest timestamp wins (position, duration, Watched); "Remove from Continue watching" is a change too, a tombstone kept 30 days. Two devices watching the same Title: the last to stop wins. Devices push after each stop and pull on launch and on coming to the front, through one `POST /v1/sync` with a cursor; offline changes queue. Pulled rows land as the Pending catalog lands (ADR 0011): between screens, never under focus; a Title page reads the latest position, so Play continues from where another TV stopped.
- **Profiles.** Up to 5 per household, name only, no avatars, on a device with no Account too; signing in merges them into the Account's by name (a Kids one merging into a non-Kids one keeps Kids), adds the rest, and asks which to keep past 5. No picker on launch: the TV stays on the last profile. With one profile nothing changes anywhere. With two or more, the current profile's name sits as a glass chip at the right end of the top tabs, where the Catalog pill sits (the pill shows left of it while visible); OK opens "Who's watching?" over the screen (names, Kids ones marked, "Add a profile"). A new profile starts with the device's language as Content language and App language. Deleting a profile deletes its history everywhere after a confirmation on the TV; the last one can't be deleted. Settings gains **Account** (Sign in / Signed in as, Devices, Sign out) and **Profiles** (the list, Add, Kids, Change PIN).
- **Kids.** A **Kids profile** is a profile marked Kids when created. Its library is the **Kids catalog**: a Title is in it when any of its Listings sits in a Category whose name has a Kids word of the Base table's genre list, which gains the languages the survey showed (INFANTILES, FAMILIAS, ΠΑΙΔΙΚΑ, ΠΑΙΔΙΑ, COCUK, ÇOCUK, BARN, BØRN, کارتون, DESSINS ANIMÉS); ANIMEE stays out (French panels file teen anime there); a Provider table can add or empty a word. Measured on the 2026-10-24 catalog: 27 movie and 20 show Categories, 2,124 Movies (3.9%) and 909 Shows (6.1%), `EN - FAMILY` being Dumbo and Marmaduke with one borderline anime. While TMDB is there its Animation and Family genres add to it; the rule needs no cloud, survives M6 and is free (ADR 0013). Nothing is hidden from the other profiles by it. Home: the hero from the Kids catalog, Continue watching, New for kids, Movies for kids, Shows for kids, then TMDB genre rows over the Kids catalog; no Charts. Movies and Shows: the same for their kind. Live: the profile's Favourites, then the Channels of Kids Categories as one row per country; no sports hub, no Channel browser. No Trailer row (YouTube's end screen shows what it likes), no Versions screen, Settings limited to Text size; Play still picks the Best playable Version and the player is unchanged; Search covers the Kids catalog. Leaving a Kids profile for any other asks the **Household PIN**, the app's only lock: 4 digits on a digit pad, set when the first Kids profile is created, five wrong tries wait 30 s, kept hashed on the device and in the Account; a Kids profile copies the creating profile's Content languages. Forgotten: the phone resets it through a magic link.
- **Onboarding and the first screen.** First launch: the Provider login, then the first Sync's checklist as today with one skippable card under it, "Sign in to Zugig to keep your history across devices", showing the QR and code inline, since the two-minute wait is when a phone is in hand (Back or Home dismisses it; Settings keeps the row), then Home. No tour, no language picker. The login screen's address field also takes an M3U URL and pulls the username and password out of it; a phone-typing path over the home network with no cloud (the stick serving a page) is a follow-up. The household member who didn't set up meets only the profile chip, which pulses once the first time a second profile is created.
- **The cloud, in D1.** `account` (id, email, created_at); `device` gains `account_id` and `profile_id` (its current profile) and keeps its token hash; `sign_in_code` (code, device token hash, expires_at) and `magic_link` (token hash, email, code, expires_at); `profile` (id, account_id, name, kids, pin_hash, settings JSON); `progress` (profile_id, title_key or Listing key with the Provider hash, season, episode, position, duration, watched, updated_at, removed_at); `favourite` and `not_this_game` per profile and Provider hash; `provider_table` (provider, prefix, attribute, value, reads, agreeing, learned_at, curated). `file_read` gains `prefix` and `hints`. The Worker serves the sign-in page and the API under the app's own domain.
- **Not planned:** passwords or third-party sign-in, the Provider login in the Account, a profile picker on launch, avatars, age ratings (the Provider's `age` is empty and `is_adult` 0%; TMDB's certifications leave with M6), a parent-built allow-list, Trakt.

## M6: Without TMDB

Settled 2026-11-07 in a grilling session against two surveys (below), the cloud's own record of TMDB's name lookups, the code as #421 left it, and a Home mock built from the Provider's art alone. The goal: before the first sale, the app and the cloud stop calling TMDB and keep none of its data (a paid app is commercial use under TMDB's terms, 149 USD a month, which a one-time price can't carry), and the look of M4 survives on what the Provider itself offers. [ADR 0014](adr/0014-no-tmdb-api-titles-keep-the-providers-number.md) amends ADR 0002 and supersedes ADR 0009. The terms are in CONTEXT.md: Title number, Unmatched Title, Mismatch, Trending.

- **The key stays, the API goes.** Titles stay grouped by the number the Provider files each Listing under (`tmdb`: TMDB's numbering, which the Provider copies), now the **Title number**, read from the catalog and never looked up. Nothing else groups the catalog: names collapse 144,031 movie Listings to 98,525 Titles, the number to 54,658, and 44% of Titles with both EN and FR Listings have French names sharing no word with the English. Subtitle services are searched by the same number, and by the IMDb ID where Wikidata gives one (75%).
- **Nothing is looked up by name.** A Listing without a number is an **Unmatched Title**, one card of its own, as today. TMDB's search found one in seven of them (14% of 7,856 movie names, 15% of 3,749 show names, from D1), Wikidata's finds fewer (6% and 3%), and half of them are Sports replays and music videos anyway. A **Mismatch** (a wrong number) is told by its year: a Listing dated more than a year from its siblings' becomes an Unmatched Title (181 of 19,148 multi-Listing Titles in the survey, real ones: `EN - Pose (2025)` with `PL - Poza (1970)`).
- **Art is the Provider's, and its links are loaded as given.** 74% of posters and every backdrop are TMDB-hosted files the Provider links to; its own host mirrors one in eight. The app loads the URL the Provider gives, as every Xtream player does, and the question is on the lawyer's list for M7. A Title's **poster** is the file most of its Listings share, ties broken by the App language's Listing, then the English one, then the Best playable Version's (41% of multi-Listing Movies have five or more posters). A Title's **backdrop** is the Provider's: a Show's from its Listing at Sync (83% have one, 63% four), a Movie's from its details (129 of 130); one that isn't 16:9 is a poster handed over as a backdrop and is refused. 90% of Movies' and 74% of Shows' are textless. **No logo exists**: a Title is named in type everywhere a logo was.
- **The look.** The **hero** is the backdrop with the name in display type, the metadata line, two lines of the Provider's plot, Play and Details; it draws a Title with a 16:9 backdrop, in a Content language, not in Continue watching, from Trending else from New on your service, a different one each entry, never changing while the Viewer is there. The **Title page** sits on the backdrop with the name in display type; a Title with no 16:9 backdrop keeps the ground and shows its poster at the right. "While a stream starts" shows the backdrop and name. Continue watching's wide still is the backdrop. **Posters stay posters**: the grown card (poster to textless backdrop on focus) is retired; a focused poster lifts 1.12× and carries its own printed name. The Top rows, their numerals and the rank on Search results and Title pages are retired with the Charts.
- **Details come from the Provider, only when a page opens.** `get_vod_info` (plot, genre, release date, duration, backdrop, director; cast on 66%, trailer key on 53%) and `get_series_info` (Episode stills on 95%) are read when the Title page opens, as the bitrate and Episodes already are, and kept for good. No call on focus: the info under a focused Home row is one line, year · genre · the Provider's rating ("★ 6.6", on 94% of Listings), with the compact badges; no description, no runtime. A Show's genre, plot, cast and rating are on its Listing; a Movie's genre comes from its Category where the Prefix table names one, else from its details. So M6 adds no Provider load beyond one `get_vod_info` for the hero's Movie per entry (174 ms median from the Mac, 138 of 138 calls answered 200); the first ticket measures it on the stick. Trailers play when the Provider has a key, and the Trailer action hides otherwise. Episodes list what the Provider has, with its stills; nothing upcoming.
- **Wikidata, for two things only** (CC0): a Title's original language, which keeps Prefer original audio and the Untagged Version's Likely (63–73% of Titles, 93–100% of the big ones; the rest stay unknown until a Header read Confirms them), and its English and French Wikipedia article names, for Trending. One cloud endpoint takes up to 300 Title numbers and answers original language, IMDb ID and article names, about 60 bytes a Title, cached in D1 for good; the device asks after a Sync in batches and keeps the answers with its catalog (a few MB for 69k Titles; 230 calls the first time, most already cached once any Viewer of a Provider has asked). Names in the App language need no Wikidata: a Title is named by its Listing in that language, else the English one, else any.
- **Trending** replaces the Charts: the cloud's cron reads Wikipedia's daily top 1,000 articles for English and each App language asked for (one call a day per wiki, pageviews are CC0), keeps seven days, and serves the article names with their views; the device sums the week, matches them to its Titles' article names and keeps what the Provider has in Content languages. Yesterday's English list held 80 of the Provider's Titles (Jailer 3, Black Doves, MobLand, Slow Horses), the French 51. Unranked, no numerals. Home: Continue watching, Trending, New on your service, Favourites, genres; Movies and Shows: Trending of the kind, New, genre rows. **New on your service** is newest first by `added`, ties by the Provider's rating. **Genre rows** come from the Prefix table's genre mapping of the Provider's genre field and Category names, ordered by how many Titles the Provider has in each, with the same minimum size; Movie genre rows start thin and fill as details arrive.
- **Search** runs on the device alone: the Provider's names in every language it carries, and the cast the Provider's details name (Shows at Sync, Movies once opened). Person search runs over that index. The cloud search endpoint goes.
- **The cloud afterwards**: `/v1/metadata`, `/v1/match`, `/v1/search`, `/v1/season`, `/v1/upcoming` and `/v1/charts` are removed with their D1 tables and the TMDB key; the Wikidata endpoint and the trending feed are added; devices, file reads, unknown Prefixes, subtitles and Games stay.
- **Transition.** The first launch after the M6 release drops the TMDB metadata, match and chart rows and clears the image cache once; the Provider's links keep working, only TMDB's curated files vanish. The "from TMDB" line and logo go; Settings gets "Data from Wikidata and Wikipedia".
- **DESIGN.md changes for the tickets**: the hero and Title page name a Title in display type; `TitleCard` loses the grown state and `CardLogo`; `RankedCard` and the numerals retire; the rail info becomes one line plus badges; the Title page without a backdrop shows the poster at the right.
- **Assumption for M5's any-Provider**: Xtream panels generally copy TMDB's numbering into `tmdb`; a Provider that doesn't gives name-only Titles, one card per Listing.
- **Not planned**: a name lookup on any source, TVmaze for upcoming Episodes (CC BY-SA, share-alike untested), fanart.tv for logos (terms unreachable), re-serving art through the cloud, a Provider call on focus.

Lost, plainly: title logos and TMDB's curated textless posters, the grown card, the Top rows, TMDB's trending and Discover, upcoming Episodes, popularity ordering, search by any-language name beyond what the Provider's own Listings carry, and the original language for the quarter of Titles Wikidata doesn't know.

## M7: Paying

Settled 2026-11-06 in a grilling session against the code, the cloud README, ADRs 0001–0012 and TMDB's, Paddle's and TiviMate's public pages. The goal: hundreds of households, on any Provider, pay once. Costs are kept in [running-costs.md](running-costs.md); the terms are in CONTEXT.md under Paying.

- **Price and unit.** 29.99 CAD, once. No subscription, no second SKU, no add-on devices. Paddle is the merchant of record (about 5% + 0.50 USD a sale; it files GST/HST and foreign tax). A Purchase belongs to the Account (M5) and Unlocks up to 3 devices signed in; signing in a fourth asks which to sign out; Settings lists the devices. TiviMate's lifetime licence, the comparison every buyer makes, covers 5 devices for 20–40 USD.
- **Paid features: the sports hub's schedules, subtitle search and download, and shared Header reads.** Everything on-device stays free: Sync, Matching, Versions and Backups, playback, Continue watching, Favourites, Prefer original audio, Background reading, the Quality cap, and trailers (the Provider's key, M6). The free tier is the full clean library on any Provider; the learned Provider table is free too, so the free app never looks like it knows nothing on a new Provider.
- **The gate is the cloud, not the APK** ([ADR 0013](adr/0013-paid-features-enforced-by-the-cloud.md)): the repo is public, so a client check would be removed in one commit. A device that isn't Unlocked is refused the paid endpoints. A Locked feature shows a real teaser the cloud does serve, and locks the act: Live keeps Channels and Favourites and shows one row per League with counts ("NHL · 6 Games tonight") and no Games, since Playing a Feed is on-device and couldn't be enforced once the Games show; subtitle offers show and the download is Locked; a free device Header reads by itself instead of getting other households' results. **Locked** becomes a state in DESIGN.md's focus grammar: focusable, a lock mark where a Likely badge would sit, OK opens the Unlock screen; distinct from Disabled, which does nothing.
- **Trial.** 14 days from the first Sync landing, during which everything is Unlocked. One per device, keyed on a SHA-256 of ANDROID_ID sent at registration: fixed per device and signing key since Android 8, it survives a reinstall and only a factory reset changes it. Device registration is rate-limited per IP with a Cloudflare rule. The Provider login is never fingerprinted (ADR 0001 stands).
- **The Unlock screen.** A Settings row, "Unlock Zugig", whose value is the state ("Trial · 9 days left", "Unlocked · 2 of 3 devices", "Locked"), and the screen every Locked act opens: what's included, the price in the Viewer's currency fetched from the cloud, a QR and a short code the phone uses to pay or to sign in; the TV notices within seconds. No banners, no nag on launch, no countdown anywhere but Settings: a household member who didn't set the app up never meets one.
- **Refunds.** Paddle's 14-day window. A refund or chargeback revokes the Purchase; every device goes Locked at its next cloud contact; nothing on the device is deleted; the Trial doesn't restart.
- **Free-tier caps.** Per-device daily caps on the cloud, generous enough that no household notices, higher when Unlocked, so a bot can't spend the quotas.
- **Before the first sale.** The repo and its issues go private (update.json served by the Worker; ADR 0010 amended); the package (`dev.betteriptv`), repo and Worker lose "IPTV" from their names, one reinstall for the household; Source names leave the UI (kept inside for Original audio and Untagged Versions) and PRODUCT.md's positioning is reworded away from "which streaming service the copy came from"; the Netflix Top 10 Chart is dropped (Netflix's data file, read without agreement); OpenSubtitles' Pro package is bought and Subdl's terms checked; a lawyer is consulted on the IPTV exposure, the data sources and Paddle's paperwork. Team and League logos stay until the lawyer says otherwise. Checked 2026-11-06: no Provider host and no catalog dump is in the repo; fonts are Inter under the Open Font License; icons are the app's own; TMDB's attribution shows.
- **Not planned:** a subscription or yearly pass (decided against), a private repo with a client-side gate, Google Play Billing or Amazon IAP (both need store distribution, ADR 0006), a device-count upsell, a Provider-login fingerprint to catch factory resets.
